Maxwell's equations in differential form, in SI units:

### Gauss's law
**∇·E = ρ/ε₀**
Electric charge is the source of the electric field. Field lines start on positive charge and end on negative charge; a region with no net charge has no divergence.

### Gauss's law for magnetism
**∇·B = 0**
There are no magnetic monopoles. Magnetic field lines always close on themselves — whatever enters a volume must leave it.

### Faraday's law
**∇×E = −∂B/∂t**
A changing magnetic field induces a circulating electric field. The minus sign is Lenz's law: the induced field opposes the change that created it.

### Ampère–Maxwell law
**∇×B = μ₀J + μ₀ε₀ ∂E/∂t**
Currents and changing electric fields both create circulating magnetic fields. The second term (displacement current) is Maxwell's addition — it makes the equations consistent with charge conservation and predicts electromagnetic waves.

Together with the Lorentz force law, these four govern all classical electromagnetism. In vacuum (ρ = 0, J = 0) they combine into wave equations with speed 1/√(μ₀ε₀) = c.
