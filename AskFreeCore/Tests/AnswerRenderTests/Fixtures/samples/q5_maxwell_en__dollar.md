Maxwell's equations in differential form (SI units):

### Gauss's law
$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} = \frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0}$$

Electric field diverges from **charge**. The flux of E out of a closed surface equals enclosed charge over $\varepsilon_0$. Charges are sources/sinks of E.

### Gauss's law for magnetism
$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0$$

No **magnetic monopoles**. Magnetic field lines always close on themselves; there is no magnetic charge density.

### Faraday's law
$$\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t}$$

A time-varying magnetic field induces a **circulating** electric field. This is the basis of induction and transformers.

### Ampère–Maxwell law
$$\nabla \times \mathbf{B} = \mu_0 \mathbf{J} + \mu_0 \varepsilon_0 \frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t}$$

Currents and changing electric fields create **circulating** magnetic fields. The second term (displacement current) is what makes electromagnetic waves possible.

In vacuum with no sources ($\rho = 0$, $\mathbf{J} = 0$), these combine into wave equations for E and B propagating at $c = 1/\sqrt{\mu_0 \varepsilon_0}$.
