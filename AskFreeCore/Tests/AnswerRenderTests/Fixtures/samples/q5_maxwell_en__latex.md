Maxwell's equations in differential form:

### Gauss's law for electricity
$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{E} = \frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0}$$

Electric charge is the source of the electric field. **Divergence** of E at a point is proportional to the charge density there; field lines start on positive charge and end on negative charge.

### Gauss's law for magnetism
$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B} = 0$$

There are no magnetic monopoles. Magnetic field lines always form **closed loops** — whatever enters a region must leave it.

### Faraday's law
$$\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t}$$

A changing magnetic field induces a circulating electric field. The **minus sign** (Lenz's law) says the induced field opposes the change that created it.

### Ampère–Maxwell law
$$\nabla \times \mathbf{B} = \mu_0 \mathbf{J} + \mu_0 \varepsilon_0 \frac{\partial \mathbf{E}}{\partial t}$$

Both electric current density **J** and a changing electric field (the displacement current term) produce a circulating magnetic field. Maxwell's addition of the second term is what makes electromagnetic waves possible.

Together these four equations, plus the Lorentz force law, form the classical basis of electromagnetism.
